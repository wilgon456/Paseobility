# Document Types

Use the section matching the requested output. If the user names a publication,
company template, or submission guide, treat that guide as authoritative.

## Contents

- Blog post or contributed article
- Personal essay or opinion column
- Newsletter
- Report, brief, or white paper
- Proposal or internal memo
- Press release or announcement
- Speech, video, or podcast script
- Social thread
- Website or product copy
- Letter or email

## Blog post or contributed article

Use a clear promise, argument, or question rather than a broad topic label.

Recommended shape:

1. Specific opening tension, observation, or result
2. Why it matters to this reader now
3. Two to five developed sections
4. Evidence, examples, and meaningful counterpoint
5. Conclusion that advances the argument rather than repeating it

Avoid generic scene-setting, keyword-stuffed headings, and a conclusion that
only summarizes prior headings.

## Personal essay or opinion column

Center a point of view. Separate lived experience supplied by the user from
facts supported by sources.

Recommended shape:

1. Scene, claim, or contradiction
2. Personal stake
3. Development through specific moments and reflection
4. Tension or honest counterargument
5. Changed understanding or resonant final image

Never invent memories, dialogue, feelings, or biographical details. Mark gaps
with questions or neutral placeholders.

## Newsletter

Write for a recurring relationship with readers.

Recommended shape:

1. Subject line options, when requested
2. Short opening that establishes relevance
3. One central idea with scannable supporting sections
4. Practical takeaway, recommendation, or curated links
5. One clear call to action or sign-off

Match the publication's recurring voice and section names when examples exist.

## Report, brief, or white paper

Prioritize traceability over flourish.

Recommended shape:

1. Title and executive summary
2. Scope, audience, and method
3. Findings organized by decision relevance
4. Evidence and limitations
5. Recommendations with owner, priority, or next action when appropriate
6. Sources or notes

Distinguish observations, interpretations, and recommendations. Use tables only
when they improve comparison.

## Proposal or internal memo

Make the decision and tradeoffs easy to inspect.

Recommended shape:

1. Decision or request
2. Current problem and evidence
3. Proposed approach
4. Alternatives considered
5. Cost, timing, risks, and mitigations
6. Clear approval or next-step request

Do not hide uncertainty behind confident business language.

## Press release or announcement

Lead with the actual news and its concrete impact.

Recommended shape:

1. Factual headline and optional subhead
2. Dateline or opening announcement
3. Who, what, when, where, why
4. Verified detail and context
5. Approved quotation or explicit quotation placeholder
6. Availability, contact, and boilerplate when supplied

Never invent executive quotations, customer claims, dates, partnerships, or
performance numbers.

## Speech, video, or podcast script

Write for the ear rather than the page.

Recommended shape:

1. Hook that can be spoken naturally
2. Clear promise or question
3. Short sections with audible transitions
4. Examples and controlled repetition
5. Closing line and call to action

Read sentences aloud mentally. Prefer contractions and varied sentence lengths.
Add timing, stage, or visual cues only when requested.

## Social thread

Make each post useful while preserving one continuous argument.

Recommended shape:

1. First post: concrete claim or tension
2. Early post: stakes and context
3. Middle posts: one idea or example each
4. Counterpoint or limitation
5. Final post: conclusion and optional call to action

Respect the target platform's current length limits only after verifying them.
Avoid fake suspense, excessive numbering, and repeated engagement bait.

## Website or product copy

Write toward a specific user decision.

Recommended shape:

1. Literal product, service, or offer
2. User problem and differentiated value
3. Evidence or concrete capability
4. Objection handling
5. One primary action

Do not invent testimonials, customers, certifications, guarantees, or product
capabilities. Match copy length to its UI container when one is provided.

## Letter or email

Put the purpose early and make the requested response explicit.

Recommended shape:

1. Context appropriate to the relationship
2. Main message or request
3. Necessary evidence or detail
4. Deadline, next step, or response requested
5. Sign-off matching the relationship

Protect private details. Do not send without explicit confirmation.
